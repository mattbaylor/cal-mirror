AskWhen.me — draft for 2.0, September 2026

These are the terms for AskWhen.me, the request-page subscription you can turn on from inside Calendar Mirror. Calendar Mirror itself is a one-time purchase from the App Store and needs no terms beyond Apple's: it runs on your device and talks to nobody. AskWhen.me runs on a server, so there are a few things worth agreeing on. They are short because the product is.

What you get

A page at askwhen.me/yourpage where people can ask you for a time, served from a server that never sees your calendar (the privacy page says what it holds instead). Your device decides which times to offer and answers every request; the server carries the requests until it does. Nothing lands in your calendar unless your device writes it — either because you accepted, or because you sent someone a personal link and the time was still clear when they used it.

The subscription

  • Bought through Apple. Prices, renewal, cancellation and refunds are handled by the App Store under Apple's terms. We never see your payment details, and we cannot issue a refund — Apple can.
  • Three tiers, annual. Request Page is one page at your askwhen.me/… address, with a free first 90 days. Custom Subdomain adds your own name — you.askwhen.me — and more than one page. Custom Domain lets you serve your page from a domain you own. The trial is on the Request Page tier only, once per Apple Account; an upgrade is charged straight away with the unused part of the year credited, arithmetic Apple shows you before you agree.
  • Cancel any time in your Apple Account's subscription settings. Your page keeps working until the period you paid for ends.

Your page, and your address

  • Your askwhen.me/… address is yours for as long as you subscribe. A subdomain you claim is yours for as long as your subscription includes it.
  • When a subscription ends — cancelled, lapsed, or refunded — the page stops taking requests at once and shows "not currently taking requests" for a grace period of seven days. Renewing inside the grace brings the same page back at the same address, so links you have sent keep working.
  • After the grace the page, its address and anything still in its queue are deleted, and links to it stop working. A released subdomain may be claimed by somebody else. A domain you own stays yours, of course — only the pointer from it to your page is removed; you keep the DNS record and can delete it.
  • A refund reverses the purchase, so there is no grace: the page stops at once.
  • Everything you ever accepted is already in your calendar and is untouched by any of this. Nothing that was agreed depended on the page staying up.

Your own domain

To serve your page from a domain you own, you add one DNS record pointing at us; the certificate is issued for you once the record is seen. You are responsible for the domain itself — its registration, its DNS, and your right to use it. We store the hostname and check from time to time that it still points here, and nothing else about it. If the record goes away, the address stops working; your askwhen.me/… address does not.

What you agree to

  • The display name and text on your page are yours, and you will not use them to impersonate someone else.
  • You will not use a page, or the email it sends on your behalf, to send unsolicited mail, to harass, or for anything unlawful. The service sends four kinds of email and no other, and only to people who asked for a time on your page.
  • We may take down a page used for any of the above, and will say so.

What we promise, and what we don't

AskWhen.me is a small service run by one person, on infrastructure we own. We keep it up with care, and if it is down, requests wait and your calendar is unaffected — the design puts nothing that matters on the server. But we do not promise any particular uptime, and the service is provided as is, without warranty. To the extent the law allows, our liability to you is limited to what you paid for the current subscription period. Nothing here limits rights the law gives you that cannot be limited.
