Calendar Mirror vs CalendarBridge

Compared August 2026 · every price and feature below was checked against CalendarBridge’s own site on 28 August 2026. These change — check the CalendarBridge site before deciding.

Busy-block sync and scheduling pages, with a HIPAA agreement available.

CalendarBridge sells a compliance story. It offers a Business Associate Agreement on its higher tiers, which is the kind of thing a clinic or a firm needs on paper before it will let a tool near a calendar. Calendar Mirror cannot offer you a BAA, because there is no company on the other end of it.

Calendar Mirror answers the same worry differently. Instead of a contract governing what a vendor may do with your calendar, there is no vendor: the copy is made on your device and nothing is transmitted. Whether that is better depends entirely on whether you need the paperwork.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • No account, and no server holding a credential to your calendar. CalendarBridge asks for an Apple app-specific password; Calendar Mirror asks for nothing, because the calendars are already on the device.
  • $2.99 once against $48–$480 a year.
  • Faster on Apple calendars and on subscribed feeds. Their own documentation puts iCloud and .ics sources at five to ten minutes.
  • Per-event control with a tag, rather than per-rule only.
  • Duration bounds — skip anything under fifteen minutes, say.

Where CalendarBridge is the better pick

  • A HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, which some workplaces simply require.
  • Scheduling pages for sharing availability.
  • Real-time sync when the source is Google or Outlook.
  • Runs in a browser, on any OS, with no Apple device involved.
  • Keeps syncing while all your devices are off.

Use them together

Put iCloud and .ics feeds on the fast path

CalendarBridge is real-time when the source is Google or Outlook, and every 5 to 10 minutes when it is iCloud or an .ics URL — their documentation says so.

Calendar Mirror can change which of those applies. Mirror the iCloud calendar or the subscribed feed into a Google or Outlook calendar on your Mac, then let CalendarBridge use that as the source. You keep its scheduling pages and, on the higher tiers, its BAA — and the slow half of the pipeline gets replaced by something that reacts in seconds.

Bridging adds a hop: Calendar Mirror writes the copy on your device, and the other service then reads that copy on its own schedule. It needs the destination account set up on your Mac or iPhone, and the copy is one-way, like every Calendar Mirror mirror.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming later. If you need one of them, buy something else:

  • No booking or scheduling links. Nothing generates a page other people can pick a slot from.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
