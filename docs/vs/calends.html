Calendar Mirror vs Calends

Compared August 2026. Every price and feature below was checked against Calends’s own App Store listing on 17 September 2026. These change — check the Calends App Store listing before deciding.

Many calendars folded into one you can share. A different shape of the same idea.

Calends solves the other half of the problem. Where Calendar Mirror keeps pairs in sync, Calends folds many calendars into a single master calendar that lives in Apple Calendar and can be shared with anyone — deduplicated, and kept filled in the background. If what you want is one calendar to hand to your family or your assistant, that is the shape you want.

Calendar Mirror can merge, but it does not deduplicate. Point several mirrors at one destination and you get the same effect; what you do not get is Calends’ removal of the same event arriving from two sources. Going the other way, Calends is built around a master calendar rather than per-pair rules, so per-pair projection and per-event tags are not what it is for.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • $2.99 once against $14.99 once.
  • Per-pair control: each pair decides for itself how much crosses over, rather than one master calendar with one policy.
  • Filters on declined, unanswered, canceled, all-day, free, duration, title and time of day.
  • Per-event tags, for the one meeting that should behave differently from the rest.
  • Open source under MIT.

Where Calends is the better pick

  • Deduplication — the same event arriving from two sources appears once. Calendar Mirror would copy it twice.
  • A 30-day trial and Family Sharing, neither of which this app offers.
  • It is purpose-built for producing one calendar to share outward, which is a cleaner fit than assembling it from pairs.
  • Merging is what it does, so the setup for that job is simpler than configuring several mirrors by hand.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming to Calendar Mirror itself. If you need one of them, buy something else — with one exception, and it comes first:

  • No booking links — by design. Nothing writes to your calendar from a web form. AskWhen.me, a separate subscription you turn on from Calendar Mirror, serves a request page instead: someone picks a time, you accept or decline, and nothing lands until you do. How it works.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
