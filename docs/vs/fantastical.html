Calendar Mirror vs Fantastical

Compared August 2026 · every price and feature below was checked against Fantastical’s own site and App Store listing on 28 August 2026. These change — check the Fantastical site and App Store listing before deciding.

A whole calendar app that added mirroring. If you already pay for it, you may not need this one.

Start here: if you already pay for Fantastical, try its mirroring first. Flexibits added Calendar Mirroring in June 2026, it runs on your device, and it is included in a subscription you are already buying. Paying $2.99 for a second app to do a job the one you own already does would be daft.

The case for a separate utility is narrower than that. Fantastical is a calendar app — you adopt it, and mirroring comes along. Calendar Mirror is a utility that adds nothing to your screen: you keep Apple Calendar, or Fantastical, or whatever you already use, and it quietly maintains copies underneath. The concrete differences are how far ahead it mirrors, how finely you can select events, and what it costs.

Not a privacy comparison. Flexibits states that no event information is sent to its servers or saved outside your device, so mirroring in Fantastical is on-device just as it is here. The differences are range, selection and price.

The range limit is the one worth checking against your own use. Fantastical always looks two weeks back, and forward by the amount its tier allows — two weeks on Free, six months on Premium. If you mirror a work calendar so that next year’s leave shows up at home, six months may not reach far enough.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • It mirrors a year ahead by default, and up to ten. Fantastical mirrors two weeks on Free and six months on Premium, which is the difference between seeing next spring and not.
  • $2.99 once against $56.99 a year, if mirroring is all you want.
  • Filters on RSVP state, duration, all-day and free time, and per-event tags — none of which Flexibits publishes for its mirroring rules.
  • You keep whatever calendar app you already like. Nothing about your day changes.
  • Open source under MIT, so the on-device claim is checkable.

Where Fantastical is the better pick

  • It is a good calendar app, and mirroring is included rather than bought separately. If you already subscribe, that is the end of the argument.
  • Instant one-way sync in real time, on every platform it supports — where this app is only that fast on the macOS build from source.
  • Mirroring rules follow you between devices. Calendar Mirror is configured separately on each one.
  • A free tier that mirrors, if two weeks of range is enough for you.
  • Windows is stated as coming. Calendar Mirror will never run there.

Use them together

Use Fantastical, mirror with this

You can run both, and it is a reasonable setup if you like Fantastical but want more range or finer selection than its mirroring offers.

Fantastical reads the same calendars any Apple device exposes, so copies made by Calendar Mirror appear in it like any other event. Keep Fantastical as the calendar you look at, and let Calendar Mirror maintain the copies underneath — a year out, filtered on declined and cancelled meetings, or tagged event by event.

Bridging adds a hop: Calendar Mirror writes the copy on your device, and the other service then reads that copy on its own schedule. It needs the destination account set up on your Mac or iPhone, and the copy is one-way, like every Calendar Mirror mirror.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming later. If you need one of them, buy something else:

  • No booking or scheduling links. Nothing generates a page other people can pick a slot from.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
