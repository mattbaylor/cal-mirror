Calendar Mirror vs Reclaim.ai

Compared August 2026. Every price and feature below was checked against Reclaim.ai’s own site on 17 September 2026. These change — check the Reclaim.ai site before deciding.

AI scheduling for Google and Outlook. Calendar sync is a supporting feature.

These are not really the same kind of tool. Reclaim schedules: it defends focus time, places habits and tasks in your week, and adapts as the week moves. Calendar sync exists to keep those decisions honest across the accounts you use.

And for most people reading this page, the comparison ends early: Reclaim's pricing page lists Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar support. iCloud is not among them. If the calendar you need mirrored lives in iCloud, Reclaim is not an option regardless of price.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • Works with iCloud at all, which for an Apple household is the entire question.
  • No account, no server, nothing transmitted.
  • $2.99 once against $120–$264 a year for the paid tiers.
  • Unlimited mirrors, where Reclaim's free tier allows one.

Where Reclaim.ai is the better pick

  • Everything Calendar Mirror deliberately is not: automatic scheduling, habits, task placement, meeting rescheduling.
  • Team features, and analytics on where your week goes.
  • A free tier, if one sync between Google and Outlook is all you need.
  • Cross-platform and browser-based.

Use them together

Give Reclaim your iCloud calendar

Reclaim reads Google and Outlook. It does not read iCloud — so if your dentist appointments, your kids’ fixtures and your evenings live in iCloud, Reclaim cannot see them, and it will happily schedule focus time straight over the top of them.

Calendar Mirror fixes that. Add your Google account to your Mac or iPhone, mirror the iCloud calendar into a Google calendar — busy-only, if you’d rather Reclaim not know what the events are — and point Reclaim at that calendar. Reclaim now defends your real week instead of half of it, and the details never leave your device unless you choose to send them.

Bridging adds a hop: Calendar Mirror writes the copy on your device, and the other service then reads that copy on its own schedule. It needs the destination account set up on your Mac or iPhone, and the copy is one-way, like every Calendar Mirror mirror.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming to Calendar Mirror itself. If you need one of them, buy something else — with one exception, and it comes first:

  • No booking links — by design. Nothing writes to your calendar from a web form. AskWhen.me, a separate subscription you turn on from Calendar Mirror, serves a request page instead: someone picks a time, you accept or decline, and nothing lands until you do. How it works.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
