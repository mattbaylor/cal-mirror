Calendar Mirror vs SYNCDATE

Compared August 2026 · every price and feature below was checked against SYNCDATE’s own site on 28 August 2026. These change — check the SYNCDATE site before deciding.

Live sync across CalDAV, iCloud and Google, with a genuinely useful free tier.

SYNCDATE is the closest competitor here, and the one Calendar Mirror beats by the least. It syncs live when a calendar changes, supports CalDAV broadly — iCloud, Fastmail, Nextcloud, self-hosted — and filters on RSVP, all-day, free time, title and a days-and-hours window. That overlaps most of what Calendar Mirror's filters do.

Pick by where you want the work done. SYNCDATE runs on a server, which means it works with no Apple device, covers CalDAV hosts Calendar Mirror has never heard of, and has a free tier. Calendar Mirror runs on your device, which means no account and nothing transmitted. Both are defensible; they are not the same trade.

Being straight about it

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • No account and no server. This is the whole of the difference, and for some people it is the only thing that matters.
  • One payment for every feature, against a subscription for the paid tiers.
  • Duration bounds, which they do not publish.
  • Per-event overrides by tag, rather than rules that apply to a whole mirror.
  • Exchange and any other account macOS itself can talk to, without a dedicated adapter.

Where SYNCDATE is the better pick

  • A free tier that really syncs.
  • CalDAV breadth Calendar Mirror cannot match — Fastmail, Nextcloud, mailbox.org, your own server.
  • Live sync on every platform they support, where Calendar Mirror's realtime is macOS-standalone only.
  • Runs with no Apple device, and keeps running while yours are off.
  • Per-calendar rules within a single sync, on the higher tiers.

Use them together

Less to add here, honestly

SYNCDATE already reads iCloud, CalDAV and iCal feeds directly, so the bridging trick that helps with the other tools mostly is not needed.

The one gap it leaves is a calendar that exists only on your Mac — an Exchange account configured in macOS, say — which no hosted service can authenticate to. Calendar Mirror can mirror that into an iCloud or CalDAV calendar SYNCDATE does read. Beyond that, the two overlap more than they complement.

Bridging adds a hop: Calendar Mirror writes the copy on your device, and the other service then reads that copy on its own schedule. It needs the destination account set up on your Mac or iPhone, and the copy is one-way, like every Calendar Mirror mirror.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

These are not oversights, and no future release is going to quietly fix them — most are consequences of running on your device instead of a server:

  • No booking or scheduling links. Nothing generates a page other people can pick a slot from.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
