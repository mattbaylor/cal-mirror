Clockwise has shut down — what to use instead

Compared August 2026. Every price and feature below was checked against Clockwise’s own site on 17 September 2026. These change — check the Clockwise site before deciding.

Shut down on 27 March 2026. Here is what that means if you were relying on it.

Clockwise is no longer available. Its own site states the team joined Salesforce and that “Our product will no longer be available starting on March 27, 2026.” Prepaid subscriptions extending past that date were refunded on a prorated basis, and the announcement says user data was deleted rather than transferred to Salesforce.

Clockwise partnered with Reclaim to take on its users. If what you valued was the AI scheduling and focus-time defense, Reclaim is the successor they endorsed. If what you actually used was the personal-calendar sync — the part that put your personal commitments into your work calendar as busy blocks — that is a much smaller job, and it does not need a subscription.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • Still here, and small enough that there is not much to shut down: it is an app on your device, not a service with a runway.
  • Open source under MIT. If it were ever abandoned, the source is public and the build takes one command — a copy you have built cannot be switched off remotely.
  • No account to lose access to, and no data held anywhere to be deleted.
  • Does the busy-block half of what Clockwise's calendar sync did, for $2.99 once.

Where Clockwise is the better pick

  • Everything Clockwise did beyond sync — focus time, meeting moving, team scheduling — is gone, and Calendar Mirror replaces none of it.
  • Clockwise worked on Google and Outlook from a browser. Calendar Mirror is Apple-only.
  • If you want that feature set back, Reclaim is the tool Clockwise itself recommended.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming to Calendar Mirror itself. If you need one of them, buy something else — with one exception, and it comes first:

  • No booking links — by design. Nothing writes to your calendar from a web form. AskWhen.me, a separate subscription you turn on from Calendar Mirror, serves a request page instead: someone picks a time, you accept or decline, and nothing lands until you do. How it works.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
