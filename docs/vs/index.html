cal-mirror, compared

Prices and features checked against each company’s own site on 28 August 2026. They change often — follow the links before you decide anything.

Seven tools that overlap with what cal-mirror does, each with its own page. Every page says plainly where the other tool is the better buy, because a comparison that admits nothing is just an advert.

The short version. cal-mirror is a $0.99 one-time app that copies one calendar into another on Apple devices, with no account, no server, and nothing transmitted anywhere. Everything else on this list is a hosted service with a subscription — except inovex/CalendarSync, which is local like cal-mirror but has no Apple support at all.

And where it loses. No booking links, no unified calendar view, no team or admin features, Apple only, and attendees are never copied. If you need any of those, buy one of the others — the pages below say which.

How these pages are written

  • Every factual claim about another product is taken from that company’s own public site, and linked at the bottom of each page.
  • Nothing is asserted about any competitor’s internals, security or data handling beyond what they publish themselves.
  • Prices carry the date they were checked. A stale price is a false claim, so if a page looks old, treat the number as unverified and follow the link.
  • No logos, no implication of endorsement or affiliation. These are other people’s products, described neutrally.
  • Where a competitor is simply better for a job, the page says so.
One claim worth stating carefully. cal-mirror detects a calendar change and writes the copy within seconds on macOS. It does not control how fast iCloud then carries that copy to your other devices — that is Apple’s schedule, not ours. Any tool that promises a change on your Mac will appear on your phone “instantly” is describing something it does not control.
