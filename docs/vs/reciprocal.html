Calendar Mirror vs ReciproCal Sync

Compared August 2026. Every price and feature below was checked against ReciproCal Sync’s own App Store listing on 17 September 2026. These change — check the ReciproCal Sync App Store listing before deciding.

The closest match on features. Free to try, subscription or $44.99 to keep.

Feature for feature this is the closest thing to Calendar Mirror on the App Store. On-device, no account, one-way and loop-free, rules for time windows and keywords, a neutral “Busy” mask, and a menu-bar agent on Mac. If you read this site’s front page and then read theirs, you would struggle to tell which described which.

What separates them is how you pay, and how far the rules reach. ReciproCal is free for one rule, then $2.99 a month, $17.99 a year, or $44.99 once. Calendar Mirror is $2.99 once for everything. In exchange, ReciproCal syncs your rules across devices and matches on location and notes, which Calendar Mirror does not do.

Not a privacy comparison. ReciproCal Sync runs on your device, with no account and no server, exactly as Calendar Mirror does. Neither is the private one; the differences are elsewhere.

Which to pick

Where Calendar Mirror is the better pick

  • One payment of $2.99 covers every feature, against $17.99 a year or $44.99 once for theirs.
  • Unlimited mirrors from the start, where the free tier is a single rule.
  • RSVP and duration filters, and per-event tags — three kinds of selection they do not publish.
  • Background syncing on Mac is included rather than a Pro feature.
  • Open source under MIT, so the on-device claim is auditable.

Where ReciproCal Sync is the better pick

  • A free tier you can actually use, and a monthly option if you would rather not pay up front.
  • Rules that follow you between devices through your own iCloud — Calendar Mirror is configured separately on each device.
  • Matching on location and notes, not just the title.
  • A calendar view that shows exactly which events were synced, which Calendar Mirror has no equivalent of.
  • A Home Screen quick action, and a calendar view of what it synced.

What Calendar Mirror does not do, on any tier

None of these are coming to Calendar Mirror itself. If you need one of them, buy something else — with one exception, and it comes first:

  • No booking links — by design. Nothing writes to your calendar from a web form. AskWhen.me, a separate subscription you turn on from Calendar Mirror, serves a request page instead: someone picks a time, you accept or decline, and nothing lands until you do. How it works.
  • No unified calendar view. Calendar Mirror makes copies; your calendar app displays them.
  • No team, admin or SSO features. It is a single-person utility.
  • Apple only. No Windows, no Android, no web.
  • Attendees are not replicated. Apple’s EventKit has no API to set participants on an event, so a copy carries the time, and whatever else you allow, but never the guest list.
