Why it is different

Everyone else’s server reads your calendar. Ours can’t.

A booking page works by holding your calendar: you connect it once, and from that day on their server reads every event you have so it can promise a slot on the spot. That is the deal, and it is why they can say book. AskWhen.me is not a booking page. Its server is never connected to your calendar, so it cannot promise anything — it can only carry the question to the one thing that knows the answer: your device.

🔒

The server is never given your calendar

Your device works out which times to offer and sends only those. Not your events, not their titles, not your address, not your name beyond the label you choose.

✅

A request, not a reservation

Someone picks a time and says who they are. The request comes to your device, which re-checks your real calendar before anything is written. If something landed in the meantime, it says so.

How it works

Your device offers. They ask. You answer.

1

Your device chooses the times

From the calendars you pick, it works out a handful of times you are willing to be asked about — a few a day, inside the hours you say, with room around what is already there.

2

You publish those, and only those

The times go up to askwhen.me/yourpage under the name you chose. The page shows a list of times and nothing else about you.

3

Someone asks

They pick a time, say who they are, and confirm their email address. The request waits for your device — the server holds it, and no more.

4

You accept, on your device

It re-checks your calendar first. Accept, and the event is written by your device; they get a calendar file by email. Decline, and they are told the time is gone.

What the person asking sees

A list of times, a name, and a promise it is a request

Your page says whose it is and nothing else about you. The times are shown in the visitor’s own zone, the line under the name is yours, and the page says on every step that this is an offer, not a reservation — that you confirm every one.

What the server holds

Two things. Everything else stops at your device.

The design is a dead drop: times go in, requests come out, and the server is never told who you are. The privacy page says the same in full, with how long each thing is kept.

It holds

  • The times you chose to offer, as start and end — nothing about why those.
  • The name you put on the page and the line under it, the meeting title and length, and your time zone.
  • Requests from people who asked: the time, their name, their address, a note — until you answer, and 14 days at most.
  • An anonymous subscription identifier from Apple, stored hashed. It says a page is paid for, not who paid.

It never sees

  • Your calendar, or any event in it — titles, locations, attendees, notes.
  • Which calendars you chose, or how many there are.
  • Your email address, your Apple ID, or your payment details.
  • The shape of your week beyond the handful of times you offered.
With Send times

A link for one person

Calendar Mirror 2.0 can send times: one tap gives you your next three free times as a line of text, ready to paste into a message. With AskWhen.me on, that message can carry a personal link: it opens your page once, for a week, for the person you sent it to. Because you sent it, there is no email confirmation — if the time is still clear when they pick it, your device accepts it for you.

💬

Paste it anywhere

Messages, Mail, Slack. The times are worked out on your device and put on the clipboard; nothing leaves your device to do it.

🔗

One link, one person, one week

A personal link is not your page’s address. It opens once and expires, so a forwarded message does not become an open door.

🛡️

Still re-checked

Even a personal link goes through the same re-check against your real calendar. If the time is gone, they are told, and nothing is written.
