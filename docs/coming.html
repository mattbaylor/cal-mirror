What’s coming

Calendar Mirror 2.0, and a second product you can turn on from it. No date — it ships when it’s done.

Calendar Mirror stays what it is. $2.99, one purchase, no server, ever. Version 2.0 adds exactly one thing to that: the ability to turn on AskWhen.me, a separate subscription with its own server, where other people can ask you for a time.

AskWhen.me, in one paragraph

You get a page at askwhen.me/yourname. Calendar Mirror publishes the times you are willing to offer — worked out on your device, from the calendars it already sees, under rules you set. Someone opens the page, picks a time, and confirms their email address. The request shows up in Calendar Mirror on your Mac, iPhone or iPad. You accept or decline. If you accept, the app writes the event into your calendar and the other person is told. It is off by default, and until you turn it on nothing about Calendar Mirror changes.

Why it isn’t a booking page — and why that matters less than it sounds

A booking page works by holding your calendar. You connect your Google or iCloud account, the service reads it to find gaps, and when someone books, the service writes the event in. That is the deal: a stranger’s form gets to put things in your calendar because you gave the service the keys.

AskWhen.me is built the other way round. Its server is a dead drop: available times go in, requests come out. It never holds your calendar, your account, or even your email address — it cannot email you, because it does not know who you are. The only thing that can put an event in your calendar is the Calendar Mirror app on your own device, after you have said yes. Nothing on the web has write access, because nothing on the web has any access.

From the other side of the page, it is nearly the same thing. They see your available times, pick one, confirm by email, and get an answer. While their request is open the time is held for them, so two people cannot ask for the same slot; when you accept, it is theirs. The difference is one word on the button — ask instead of book — and one fact behind it: the answer comes from you, not from a form.

If what you want is for the form to write to your calendar for you, so a meeting is booked before you have seen it, a booking page is the right tool and there are good ones. If what you want is for people to stop emailing “when are you free?” while you keep the last word, this is that.

What it costs

AskWhen.me is a yearly subscription through the App Store, in three sizes. Calendar Mirror itself is not part of the subscription and does not change price.

US prices; Apple sets the equivalent in other regions. Cancel any time from your Apple subscriptions, and the page goes with it.

Privacy

AskWhen.me has its own privacy terms, and needs them, because it has a server. Calendar Mirror does not get any: it still has no account, no backend, and makes no network requests of its own until the moment you turn AskWhen.me on — and the day you turn it off, that is true again. The privacy page will carry both, side by side, when 2.0 ships.

Also in 2.0

The two changes that have been waiting on main as 1.4.2 ship inside it: real-time syncing in the Mac App Store app, and skipping duplicates when two mirrors share a destination calendar. The release notes have the detail.

When

When it is done. There is no date, and there will not be one until it is a release note. If you want to know the moment it lands, watch the repository.
